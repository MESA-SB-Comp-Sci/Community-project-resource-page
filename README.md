# Tech for Our Community — Article Bank

Each group picks **one** NYC community problem below and uses its articles to answer one question:

> **How can we, the next generation, use technology to improve our community?**

## How to use this bank

- Every topic has a problem, the tech that addresses it, and 2 articles.
- Start with the ★ article (shorter, easier). The ★★ article adds detail.
- No two groups take the same topic.
- Write what you learn in your Markdown log, and save the link to every article you use.

## 1. Reporting neighborhood problems

**Problem:** Potholes, broken streetlights, noise, no heat in winter. Residents need a way to tell the city, and the city needs to know where problems keep happening.

**Tech:** NYC 311, an app, website and phone line that sends complaints to the right city agency. Every complaint becomes public data on a map.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [City launches online map of 311 complaints](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110216/manhattan/city-launches-online-map-of-311-complaints.amp) (DNAinfo) | The city starts showing 311 complaints block by block so residents can see problems near them |
| ★★ | [What is grinding New York City's gears?](https://amsterdamnews.com/?p=10421272) (Amsterdam News) | A new state tool maps 311 complaints by neighborhood, and the data has gaps |

**Questions**

1. What kinds of problems do people report to 311?
2. How does a complaint turn into data that helps the whole neighborhood?
3. If some neighborhoods report less, does that mean they have fewer problems?

## 2. Street flooding

**Problem:** Heavy rain and high tides flood streets in neighborhoods like Gowanus, Hamilton Beach and the Rockaways. People don't know which roads are safe.

**Tech:** FloodNet, small solar-powered sensors on street poles that measure water depth and post it live to a public map.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [Flood sensors help NYC residents stay out of dangerous waters](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/flood-sensors-help-nyc-residents-stay-out-dangerous-waters) (PreventionWeb) | Short read: how the sensors work and why residents check the map |
| ★★ | [FloodNet NYC reaches 400 sensors](https://engineering.nyu.edu/news/floodnet-nyc-reaches-400-sensors-providing-citywide-view-street-level-flooding) (NYU Tandon) | How the network grew, who uses the data, and how communities helped choose sensor spots |

**Questions**

1. Who is most affected by flooding, and why?
2. How does the sensor turn a puddle into data people can use?
3. Who might not see or use the flood map?

## 3. Air quality and asthma

**Problem:** The Bronx has the highest rates of childhood asthma hospital visits in NYC. Pollution changes block by block, and official monitors are far apart.

**Tech:** Low-cost air sensors that students and community groups place at schools or carry around, mapping pollution in real time.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [Students track air pollution with AirCasting](https://www.ssbx.org/newsfeed/students-track-air-pollution-with-aircasting) (Sustainable South Bronx) | High school students walk the South Bronx with handheld sensors that build a color-coded pollution map |
| ★★ | [Citizen science project offers NY students a breath of fresh air](https://publishing.aip.org/publications/latest-content/pollution-tracking-citizen-science-project-offers-new-york-students-a-breath-of-fresh-air/) (AIP) | Fordham's FRESH Air project put sensors in Bronx schools and found some buildings let outdoor pollution in |

**Questions**

1. Why does one neighborhood need its own air data?
2. What did students do with the sensors, and what did they learn?
3. Collecting data is step one. What has to happen next for air to actually improve?

## 4. Extreme heat

**Problem:** Summer heat waves are dangerous, especially for seniors and families without air conditioning. Some neighborhoods, like Harlem, get much hotter than others.

**Tech:** Street kiosks and online maps that show the nearest open cooling center, plus cheap heat sensors placed inside apartments.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [NYC kiosks offer real-time info on cooling centers](https://www.amny.com/news/nyc-kiosks-offer-real-time-info-cool-centers/) (amNY, 2026) | LinkNYC kiosks across the city now update automatically to show open cooling centers |
| ★★ | [Harlem Heat Project tackles an ongoing problem](https://untappedcities.com/2016/07/28/harlem-heat-project-tackles-an-ongoing-problem-in-one-of-nycs-hottest-neighborhoods/) (Untapped Cities) | Journalists built heat sensors and placed them in Harlem apartments to measure indoor heat |

**Questions**

1. Why is heat more dangerous in some neighborhoods than others?
2. How do the kiosks and the sensors help in different ways?
3. A senior who doesn't go outside or use a phone: how would they get this information?

## 5. Internet access

**Problem:** Many families in public housing (NYCHA) could not afford home internet. During remote learning, students fell behind.

**Tech:** Big Apple Connect, a city program that gives free high-speed internet, a router and Wi-Fi in common areas to NYCHA residents.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [Big Apple Connect bringing free internet to hundreds of thousands](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/big-apple-connect-program-bringing-free-internet-to-hundreds-of-thousands-of-new-yorkers/) (CBS New York) | Residents like a single dad and a parent of four explain what free internet changed for their families |
| ★★ | [Adams wants to get every NYCHA resident online](https://politicsny.com/2022/09/19/adams-wants-to-get-every-nycha-resident-online-with-free-internet-and-cable/) (PoliticsNY) | Why the city launched the program, what residents get, and what the pandemic showed |

**Questions**

1. What can't you do today without internet at home?
2. Who pays for "free" internet, and could it end?
3. Is having internet enough, or do people need other help too?

## 6. Getting around the subway

**Problem:** Many subway stations have no elevator, and elevators break. For wheelchair users, people with low vision, seniors and parents with strollers, one broken elevator can ruin a trip.

**Tech:** Apps that show which stations have working elevators, and an app that reads subway signs out loud for people with low vision.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [New app Wheely will make the NYC subway more accessible](https://www.6sqft.com/new-app-wheely-will-make-the-nyc-subway-more-accessibilty-friendly/) (6sqft, 2014) | A New Yorker builds an app after his father, a wheelchair user, got stuck at a station. Numbers are from 2014; more stations have elevators now |
| ★★ | [New app for navigating subway stations](https://aao.org/eyenet/article/new-app-for-navigating-subway-stations) (EyeNet) | NYU researchers test Commute Booster, an app that finds and reads the signs a person with low vision needs |

**Questions**

1. Who has trouble using the subway, and why?
2. Wheely was built by one person with a personal reason. What does that tell you about who can make tech?
3. An app can't build an elevator. What can tech fix here, and what can't it?

## 7. Food access

**Problem:** Families who use food pantries often wait in line for hours, outside in the cold, while also juggling jobs and kids.

**Tech:** Plentiful, a free app and text-message system for reserving a food pantry pickup time. It works in nine languages and on any phone that can text.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [New app Plentiful streamlines how NYC food pantries feed the hungry](https://philanthropynewyork.org/node/22906) (Philanthropy New York) | A mother of five and pantry staff describe how reservations replaced long lines and paper records |
| ★★ | [People in need can now make food pantry reservations](https://www.thedailymeal.com/eat/people-need-can-now-make-food-pantry-reservations-using-opentable-style-app/112217) (The Daily Meal) | How the app saves hours per visit and how pantries use the data to plan |

**Questions**

1. Why is waiting in line such a big problem for these families?
2. Why did the designers make it work by text message and in nine languages?
3. What would you add to the app to help even more people?

## 8. Language access

**Problem:** About half of NYC public school families speak a language other than English at home. Many parents can't talk directly with teachers or understand city services.

**Tech:** Phone interpreters, and newer real-time translation apps that city workers and schools use to talk with families in their own language.

| Level | Article | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★ | [NYC schools seek to end "language dislocation"](https://www.voanews.com/content/nyc-schools-seek-to-end-language-dislocation/3141980.html) (VOA) | NYC schools add translation in nine languages and phone interpreters after 5 p.m. |
| ★★ | [Mayor directs city agencies to expand language technology](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2025/12/mayor-adams-issues-directive-to-city-agencies-to-identify-ways-t.html) (NYC.gov, 2025) | The city adds translation apps to work phones and plans real-time translation for school families |

**Questions**

1. Has your family ever had trouble understanding a school or city message?
2. What are the pros and cons of a translation app vs. a human interpreter?
3. How could students like you help close the language gap with tech?
